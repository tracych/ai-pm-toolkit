# Problem Statement Rubric

Eight dimensions. Score each 1–5 using the anchors below. Always cite the phrase in the input that drove the score.

1. **Specificity** — Does the statement name a concrete user, surface, and moment, or does it use vague catch-all language?
   - 1: Pure abstraction ("users have a bad experience").
   - 3: Names the user OR the surface OR the moment — not all three.
   - 5: Names user segment + specific surface/step + specific moment in the journey.

2. **User-centricity** — Is the problem framed from the user's perspective and lived experience, or from the company's internal goals?
   - 1: Framed entirely around business metrics or internal org needs ("we need to increase retention").
   - 3: Mentions users but treats them as a metric input, not a person with a goal.
   - 5: Articulates a user goal, friction, or unmet need in their own terms.

3. **Measurability** — Could a reasonable team agree on whether the problem got smaller after a change?
   - 1: No measurable dimension at all.
   - 3: Implies a measurable outcome but doesn't name a metric or signal.
   - 5: Names at least one observable behavior, metric, or signal tied to the problem.

4. **Falsifiability** — Could the underlying claim be proven wrong by evidence?
   - 1: Tautological or unfalsifiable ("users want a better product").
   - 3: A directional claim that could be tested but isn't framed as a hypothesis.
   - 5: Framed as a claim that specific evidence could refute (e.g., "if X were true we'd see Y").

5. **Scope** — Is the problem sized for a meaningful unit of work without being a boil-the-ocean ask?
   - 1: Either trivially narrow (one bug) or impossibly broad (re-platform the company).
   - 3: Sized roughly but the boundary is fuzzy — could mean a week or a year.
   - 5: Bounded by a clear unit of work (a flow, a surface, a segment, a quarter).

6. **Urgency** — Is there a stated or implied reason this matters now versus later?
   - 1: No "why now" signal at all.
   - 3: Implies urgency through tone but doesn't ground it in evidence or context.
   - 5: Names a trigger, deadline, competitive pressure, or trend that justifies prioritization.

7. **Evidence quality** — Does the statement reference data, research, or observation, and is the source legible?
   - 1: No evidence; pure assertion or anecdote.
   - 3: References data or research without naming the source, method, or sample.
   - 5: Cites a specific signal with a legible source (survey, log, interview count, behavioral metric).

8. **Falsifiable success criteria** — Does it name what "solved" looks like in a way that could be observed, not just felt?
   - 1: No success criteria, or criteria are purely subjective.
   - 3: Mentions success in directional terms ("users will be happier") without an observable signal.
   - 5: Names at least one observable outcome that would indicate the problem is meaningfully reduced.
