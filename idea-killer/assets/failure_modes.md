# Failure mode categories

The 7 mandatory categories every kill-report must cover. Use the sub-prompts to push past the obvious answer.

## Demand

How this dies: nobody actually wants it badly enough to change behavior.

- Which specific user segment, in their own words, would describe this as a top-3 unmet need? What evidence?
- What are they doing today instead? If "nothing," that's usually because the pain isn't sharp enough — argue why.
- Would they pay for it, switch for it, or just nod politely in an interview? Distinguish stated preference from revealed preference.

## Distribution

How this dies: real people never find out it exists, or finding out costs more than they're worth.

- Which channel reaches the target user? Why hasn't an incumbent already saturated that channel?
- What's the realistic CAC vs LTV? Where does the math break?
- Is this dependent on a platform (app store, search engine, social feed, OS) that can change the rules overnight?

## Competition

How this dies: somebody else — including the "do nothing" alternative — eats your lunch.

- Who are the 3 most direct competitors? What stops them from copying this in a quarter?
- What's the strongest *substitute* (not direct competitor — the spreadsheet, the email thread, the in-person workaround)?
- Why hasn't a well-resourced incumbent already done this? If the answer is "they didn't think of it," you're probably wrong.

## Regulation

How this dies: a law, policy, platform rule, or institutional gatekeeper makes it illegal, restricted, or de-platformed.

- What jurisdictions does this touch? Which existing laws (privacy, financial, health, employment, content, AI) apply?
- If this scales 100x, what new regulatory scrutiny attracts? Has a similar product been sanctioned?
- Are there platform terms of service (app stores, ad networks, API providers) that could revoke access? What's the dependency risk?

## Unit economics

How this dies: the math doesn't work at scale, even if everything else works.

- Cost to serve one user per month. Revenue per user per month. Gross margin. Be specific with rough numbers.
- What's the biggest variable cost? What happens to margin if it doubles?
- Payback period on CAC. If >18 months for a startup, why is that survivable?
- Is the pricing model fighting a stronger gravitational pull (e.g., free incumbents, commoditized inputs, race to the bottom)?

## Organization / team

How this dies: the people building it can't actually build, sell, or operate it — or won't stay long enough to.

- Does the team have direct experience in this domain, channel, and buyer type? If not, what's the learning curve cost?
- What's the org's tolerance for the time-to-revenue this idea requires? Does that match the funding runway / quarterly pressure?
- What internal politics, incentives, or competing priorities would starve this idea after launch?
- Operational complexity at scale — support load, content moderation, compliance ops, on-call. Who runs that?

## Timing

How this dies: it's too early (no enabling tech, no user habit, no distribution channel) or too late (saturated, commoditized, regulated).

- Why now? What changed in the last 18 months that makes this possible/desirable now and not 3 years ago?
- Who tried this before and failed? Why? What's different this time, specifically?
- What would have to be true in 2 years for this to *still* be a good bet? If those things are uncertain, that's timing risk.
- Is there a window — and how wide is it before incumbents or commoditization closes it?
